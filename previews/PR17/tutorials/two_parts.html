Back and belly: two halves ​

The back of an animal faces the sun and the sky, and its belly faces the ground, usually with thinner fur. This tutorial splits a body into a dorsal and a ventral half, the commonest body of more than one part, and then adds a head.

Why split the body ​

A single shape has one coat and one set of surroundings. The way around this in NicheMapR is to solve the heat budget twice, for an animal that is all back under the sky and one that is all belly over the ground, and to average the two answers. With two halves joined into one body, each half has its own coat and its own view, and heat can pass between them, as solved by HeatExchange.jl.

Two halves ​

A HalfEllipsoid is an ellipsoid cut lengthwise. The mass is that of the half:

julia
using BiophysicalGeometry, Unitful

mass = 500.0u"g"
density = 1000.0u"kg/m^3"
back_fur = FibrousLayer(10.0u"mm", 20.0u"μm", 8000u"cm^-2")
belly_fur = FibrousLayer(3.0u"mm", 20.0u"μm", 8000u"cm^-2")

dorsal = Body(HalfEllipsoid(; mass = mass / 2, density, axis_ratio_b = 3.0, axis_ratio_c = 6.0), back_fur)
ventral = Body(HalfEllipsoid(; mass = mass / 2, density, axis_ratio_b = 3.0, axis_ratio_c = 6.0), belly_fur)

They are joined over the whole of their flat faces:

julia
animal = CompositeBody(;
    parts = (; dorsal, ventral),
    joins = (Join(dorsal = Attachment(Flat(), FullCover()), ventral = Attachment(Flat(), FullCover())),),
)

parts names the two bodies, and the Join says where each is attached: at its Flat face, with the whole face covered. The first part listed, dorsal, stays where it is and ventral is placed against it.

Areas ​

The flat faces are hidden, so the area of the body is that of the two domes:

julia
map(x -> uconvert(u"cm^2", x), (; whole = total_area(animal), dorsal = total_area(dorsal), ventral = total_area(ventral),
                                 join = join_area(animal.joins[1], animal)))
(whole = 461.5174705945705 cm^2, dorsal = 368.90650442930877 cm^2, ventral = 312.29974929949026 cm^2, join = 109.84439156711426 cm^2)

Sun, sky and ground ​

With the sun overhead, only the back is lit:

julia
map(x -> uconvert(u"cm^2", x), silhouette(animal, Beam(0.0, 0.0, 1.0)))
(dorsal = 155.62463154148227 cm^2, ventral = 0.0 cm^2)

and with the sun low, both halves are:

julia
map(x -> uconvert(u"cm^2", x), silhouette(animal, Beam(1.0, 0.0, 0.3)))
(dorsal = 59.173984014766035 cm^2, ventral = 6.96319343378264 cm^2)

Over flat ground the back sees mostly sky and the belly mostly ground:

julia
views = silhouette_factors(animal, Sky(0.5))
(; dorsal = views.dorsal, ventral = views.ventral)
(dorsal = (sky = 0.4995544898983092, ground = 0.18268887330246694, neighbours = (dorsal = 0.0, ventral = 0.3177566367992239)), ventral = (sky = 0.09427750922074368, ground = 0.5004820896203478, neighbours = (dorsal = 0.4052404011589084, ventral = 0.0)))

The rest of the view of each half is the other half, across the join. The split between sky and ground is not complete, as the sides of each half see past the horizontal. An average of an all-back and an all-belly animal assumes that it is.

Adding a head ​

Other parts are joined to either half. Here a sphere is joined to the front of the dorsal half over a small disc. A point on the dome is given by two angles, from the long axis and around it:

julia
head = Body(Sphere(; mass = 60.0u"g", density), back_fur)
patch = Disc(1.0u"cm")
with_head = CompositeBody(;
    parts = (; dorsal, ventral, head),
    joins = (
        Join(dorsal = Attachment(Flat(), FullCover()), ventral = Attachment(Flat(), FullCover())),
        Join(dorsal = Attachment(Dome(0.25, π / 2), patch), head = Attachment(Radial(π / 2, 0.0), patch)),
    ),
)

The body is now a graph of three parts and two joins, see Bodies as graphs. The head takes some of the sun from the back:

julia
map(x -> uconvert(u"cm^2", x), silhouette(with_head, Beam(1.0, 0.0, 1.0)))
(dorsal = 93.21966290703783 cm^2, ventral = 0.0 cm^2, head = 36.908443186079204 cm^2)

Half cylinders ​

A HalfCylinder is split in the same way. Like every shape it lies along x with its flat face down, so the two halves meet as they are, see Joins and poses:

julia
torso = CompositeBody(;
    parts = (; dorsal = Body(HalfCylinder(; mass = mass / 2, density, axis_ratio_b = 3.0), back_fur),
               ventral = Body(HalfCylinder(; mass = mass / 2, density, axis_ratio_b = 3.0), belly_fur)),
    joins = (Join(dorsal = Attachment(Flat(), FullCover()), ventral = Attachment(Flat(), FullCover())),),
)
