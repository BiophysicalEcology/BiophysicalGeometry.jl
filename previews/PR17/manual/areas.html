Areas and volumes ​

The area and volume functions work on a single Body and on a CompositeBody. For a body of several parts they sum over the parts, and take off the patches hidden by the joins.

One part ​

julia
using BiophysicalGeometry, Unitful

density = 1000.0u"kg/m^3"
fur = FibrousLayer(10.0u"mm", 30.0u"μm", 3000u"cm^-2")
fat = FatLayer(0.1, 901.0u"kg/m^3")
torso = Body(Cylinder(; mass = 20.0u"kg", density, axis_ratio_b = 3.0), CompositeInsulation(fur, fat))
(; total = total_area(torso), skin = skin_area(torso), evaporation = evaporation_area(torso),
   flesh_volume = flesh_volume(torso), volume = torso.geometry.volume)
(total = 0.5235114201167448 m^2, skin = 0.4575414983798043 m^2, evaporation = 0.4478389875621527 m^2, flesh_volume = 0.017780244173140954 m^3, volume = 0.02 m^3)
FunctionArea or volume
total_areathe outside of the body, over the fur
skin_areathe skin
evaporation_areathe skin between the fibres
surface_area(shape, body, surface)one named surface, such as EndA()
flesh_volumethe volume inside the fat
body.geometry.volumethe whole volume inside the skin

Several parts ​

Each join hides a patch on both of its parts:

julia
head = Body(Ellipsoid(; mass = 2.0u"kg", density, axis_ratio_b = 1.5, axis_ratio_c = 1.5), fur)
patch = Disc(4.0u"cm")
join = Join(torso = Attachment(EndB(0.0u"m", 0.0), patch), head = Attachment(PoleB(), patch))
body = CompositeBody(; parts = (; torso, head), joins = (join,))

total_area(body), total_area(torso) + total_area(head) - 2 * join_area(join, body)
(0.6136290304808778 m^2, 0.6136290304808778 m^2)

Volumes add, as nothing is lost at a join:

julia
flesh_volume(body), flesh_volume(torso) + flesh_volume(head)
(0.019780244173140955 m^3, 0.019780244173140955 m^3)

The patch is taken at its full size from the total area, the skin area and the evaporation area alike.

Halves make a whole ​

Two halves joined over the whole of their flat faces have the area and volume of the shape they were cut from:

julia
dorsal = Body(HalfCylinder(; mass = 10.0u"kg", density, axis_ratio_b = 3.0), Naked())
ventral = Body(HalfCylinder(; mass = 10.0u"kg", density, axis_ratio_b = 3.0), Naked())
halves = CompositeBody(; parts = (; dorsal, ventral), joins = (
    Join(dorsal = Attachment(Flat(), FullCover()), ventral = Attachment(Flat(), FullCover())),))
whole = Body(Cylinder(; mass = 20.0u"kg", density, axis_ratio_b = 3.0), Naked())

total_area(halves), total_area(whole)
(0.4575414983798043 m^2, 0.4575414983798043 m^2)

The halves need not match. Thick fur above and thin fur below:

julia
furry_back = Body(HalfCylinder(; mass = 10.0u"kg", density, axis_ratio_b = 3.0), FibrousLayer(30.0u"mm", 30.0u"μm", 3000u"cm^-2"))
thin_belly = Body(HalfCylinder(; mass = 10.0u"kg", density, axis_ratio_b = 3.0), FibrousLayer(5.0u"mm", 30.0u"μm", 3000u"cm^-2"))
two_coats = CompositeBody(; parts = (; furry_back, thin_belly), joins = (
    Join(furry_back = Attachment(Flat(), FullCover()), thin_belly = Attachment(Flat(), FullCover())),))

Part by part ​

The parts of a composite are ordinary bodies, so any function can be mapped over them:

julia
map(total_area, body.parts)
(torso = 0.5235114201167448 m^2, head = 0.10017070685562034 m^2)

Functions that describe a single shape, such as skin_radius, return the value for the root part when given a composite.
