Frustums, truncation and halves ​

Limbs taper, heads sit flush against necks, and backs differ from bellies. Three variations on the basic shapes allow for this: a cone cut short, an ellipsoid with one end sliced off, and a shape cut in half lengthwise.

Cones and frustums ​

A frustum is a cone with its tip cut off parallel to the base. Cone takes a top_ratio, the radius of the top over the radius of the base: 0 is a sharp cone, and values up to 1 are frustums that approach a cylinder.

julia
using BiophysicalGeometry, Unitful

m, density = 1.0u"kg", 1000.0u"kg/m^3"

The mass is the same in each, so a sharper cone is wider and longer:

julia
leg = Body(Cone(m, density, 2.5, 0.4), Naked())
leg.geometry.length, total_area(leg)
((radius_skin = 0.049654535827584945 m, length_skin = 0.24827267913792472 m), 0.06359493276037817 m^2)

radius_skin is the radius of the base, and the top has radius top_ratio * radius_skin.

Truncated ellipsoids ​

Ellipsoid takes a fifth argument, pole_a_truncation: the fraction of the long semi-axis sliced off the +x end, leaving a flat disc. At 0 the ellipsoid is whole, and at 1 it is cut through the middle.

The disc is where another part is joined, such as a head onto a neck. Its radius follows from the truncation: for a disc of radius f times the short semi-axis, the truncation is 1−1−f2.

julia
fraction = 0.7
head = Body(Ellipsoid(m, density, 2.0, 1.0, 1 - sqrt(1 - fraction^2)), Naked())
surface_area(head.shape, head, PoleA()), π * (fraction * skin_radius(head))^2
(0.0037319305185449787 m^2, 0.0037319305185449787 m^2)

Truncation changes where a part can be joined and how it is drawn. The dimensions, area and volume are still those of the whole ellipsoid, so truncations should be small.

Halves ​

HalfCylinder, HalfEllipsoid and HalfSphere are a shape cut lengthwise through its centre, with a flat face where it was cut. Two halves can then have different layers, such as thick fur on the back and thin fur on the belly.

The mass given is that of the half. A half has the dimensions of the whole shape of twice its mass, half its volume, and half its area plus the flat face:

julia
whole = Body(Cylinder(2m, density, 2.0), Naked())
half = Body(HalfCylinder(m, density, 2.0), Naked())
flat_face = surface_area(half.shape, half, Flat())
total_area(half), total_area(whole) / 2 + flat_face
(0.06962646295582982 m^2, 0.06962646295582982 m^2)

Each of these is a Half wrapped around the whole shape, so use mass to get its mass:

julia
mass(half.shape), mass(half.shape.parent)
(1.0 kg, 2.0 kg)

Two halves joined on their flat faces make the whole shape again, see Areas and volumes.
